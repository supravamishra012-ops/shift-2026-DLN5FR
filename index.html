Green Plate

Cities want cleaner streets. Operators want riders. Old batteries need somewhere to go. Nobody has one map for all three.

We kept running into the same gap. A municipal officer asks which wards are ready for shared e-bikes, and gets a spreadsheet. An operator asks which city will give them parking and permits, and gets a meeting. Neither asks what happens to the batteries in five years. Green Plate puts the VAHAN registration data, a battery waste forecast and a street-level siting score on one map, then turns it into a pilot plan both sides can sign.

Citiesdon't know where shared e-mobility will actually get used, or how much land and parking to set aside.
Operatorspick launch zones by gut feel and spend months negotiating permits zone by zone.
Batteriesfrom today's e-rickshaws and scooters will reach end of life by 2030, and few districts have a collection plan.
Both sides see the same map and the same plan. Your choice only decides which notes come first.
